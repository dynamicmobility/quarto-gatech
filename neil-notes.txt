

Overview:
This paper shows that "muscle synergy priors" enhance biomechanical fidelity, specifically in muscoluskeletal simulation. The paper extracts low-dimensional muscle synergy basis from inverse musculoskeletal analyses of overground walking trials. They then create a 3-dimensional muscle driven model and train it across various speeds and uneven terrain using RL. Overall, the paper shows that embedding neurophysiological structure in RL can improve biomechanical fidelity.

The problem:
Advances in RL have enabled msm sims to generate diverse, human-like movements and perform challenging locomotion tasks in various envs. Much of this progress has been driven by motion imitation RL.
However, these policies primarily focus on replicating reference kinematics rather than truly imitate the underlying neuromuscular coordination and joint kinetics. Moreover, these policies can be brittle or not transferable to out-of-distribution conditions and environments.
